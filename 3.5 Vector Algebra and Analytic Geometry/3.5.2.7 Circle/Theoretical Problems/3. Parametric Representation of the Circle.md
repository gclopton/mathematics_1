

### 3. Parametric Representation of the Circle


#### Exercise 1 - Build the parametrization from first principles

> [!question]
> Start with the unit circle ( $\cos t, \sin t$ ). Show that scaling by $R$ and translating by $C=\left(x_0, y_0\right)$ yields $x(t)=x_0+R \cos t, y(t)=y_0+R \sin t$. Interpret this as the action of a rotation $R_t$ on the unit vector $(1,0)$, followed by scaling and translation, and justify why every such rigid motion preserves circularity.

#### Exercise 2 - Equivalence with the Cartesian equation

> [!question]
> Substitute $x(t), y(t)$ into $\left(x-x_0\right)^2+\left(y-y_0\right)^2=R^2$ and prove the identity using $\cos ^2 t+\sin ^2 t=1$
> . Explain why no extraneous points are introduced and why every point on the parametric image lies on the Cartesian circle.

#### Exercise 3 - Surjectivity and periodicity

> [!question]
> Fix a point ( $x, y$ ) on the circle and prove there exists $t=\operatorname{atan} 2\left(y-y_0, x-x_0\right)$ with ( $x-x_0, y- \left.y_0\right)=R(\cos t, \sin t)$. Conclude that the parametrization is surjective and that $t \sim t+2 \pi$ traces the circle exactly once (up to starting phase). State precisely the role of the branch cut for atan2.

#### Exercise 4 - Orientation

> [!question]
> Show that as $t$ increases, the parametrized point moves counterclockwise. One way: evaluate the signed area integral $\frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2 \pi}\left(x y^{\prime}-y x^{\prime}\right) d t$ and show it is $+\pi R^2$, which fixes the orientation.

#### Exercise 5 - Tangent orthogonality to the radius

> [!question]
> Differentiate $\left(x(t)-x_0\right)^2+\left(y(t)-y_0\right)^2=R^2$ with respect to $t$ to obtain $\left(x-x_0\right) x^{\prime}+(y- \left.y_0\right) y^{\prime}=0$. Conclude that the velocity $\left(x^{\prime}, y^{\prime}\right)$ is orthogonal to the radius vector $\left(x-x_0, y-y_0\right)$ at every $t$. Verify the same fact directly from $x^{\prime}(t)=-R \sin t, y^{\prime}(t)=R \cos t$.


#### Exercise 6 - Tangent line from the parametrization

> [!question]
> Fix $t_0$. Derive the tangent line at $P=\left(x\left(t_0\right), y\left(t_0\right)\right)$ as $\left(x-x_0\right) \cos t_0+\left(y-y_0\right) \sin t_0=R$. Reduce this to $x x_1+y y_1=R^2$ in the special case $x_0=y_0=0$ with $P=\left(x_1, y_1\right)$, and explain the equivalence with the implicit-gradient method.

#### Exercise 7 - Constant speed and arc length

> [!question]
> Compute the speed $v(t)=\sqrt{x^{\prime}(t)^2+y^{\prime}(t)^2}$ and prove $v(t)=R$ is constant. Integrate $s(t)=\int v d t$ to show $s\left(t_2\right)-s\left(t_1\right)=R\left(t_2-t_1\right)$. Deduce that $t$ is an arc-length parameter up to the constant scale $R$.

#### Exercise 8 - Reparametrization by arc length

> [!question]
> Define $s=R t$ and rewrite the circle as $x(s)=x_0+R \cos (s / R), y(s)=y_0+R \sin (s / R)$. Prove that $\|(d x / d s, d y / d s)\|=1$ and interpret this as unit-speed motion along the circle.

#### Exercise 9 - Curvature is $1 / R$

> [!question]
> Using $x^{\prime}(t), y^{\prime}(t), x^{\prime \prime}(t), y^{\prime \prime}(t)$, evaluate $\kappa(t)=\frac{\left|x^{\prime} y^{\prime \prime}-y^{\prime} x^{\prime \prime}\right|}{\left(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2}\right)^{3 / 2}}$ and show $\kappa \equiv 1 / R$. Conclude that the center of curvature at every point is the fixed center $C$.

#### Exercise 10 - Regularity and non-self-intersection

> [!question]
> Show that $\left(x^{\prime}(t), y^{\prime}(t)\right) \neq(0,0)$ for all $t$, hence the map is a regular immersion. Prove that $\left(x\left(t_1\right), y\left(t_1\right)\right)=\left(x\left(t_2\right), y\left(t_2\right)\right)$ implies $t_1-t_2 \in 2 \pi \mathbb{Z}$, so the trace has no self-intersections.

#### Exercise 11 - Compatibility with rigid motions

> [!question]
> Let $Q$ be any $2 \times 2$ orthogonal matrix and $T \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Show that $X(t)=Q(C+R(\cos t, \sin t))+T$ parametrizes a circle of the same radius, with center $Q C+T$. Explain how this underpins the "rotate-scale-translate" construction in Exercise 1.

#### Exercise 12 - Line integrals on the circle

> [!question]
> Evaluate $\oint_{\mathcal{C}}(x d y-y d x)$ using the parametrization and confirm that the result is $2 \pi R^2$. Deduce the signed area $\pi R^2$ via Green's theorem, reinforcing the orientation result from Exercise 4.

#### Exercise 13 - When angle is not the parameter

> [!question]
> Let $u \mapsto t(u)$ be $C^1$ and strictly monotone. Show that $d s / d u=R\left|t^{\prime}(u)\right|$. Characterize precisely when $u$ is proportional to arc length, and give an explicit example where nonuniform $t(u)$ yields variable speed around the circle.
