
### 4. Equation of the circle in polar coordinates



#### Exercise 1 - Law of cosines to general polar form

> [!question]
> With $P=(\rho, \varphi)$ and $C=\left(\rho_0, \varphi_0\right)$, form triangle $O C P$. Apply the law of cosines at $O$ to derive
> 
> $$
> R^2=\rho^2+\rho_0^2-2 \rho \rho_0 \cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)
> $$
> 
> then rearrange to
> 
> $$
> \rho^2-2 \rho \rho_0 \cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)+\rho_0^2=R^2
> $$
> 
> 
> State clearly where the angle $\varphi-\varphi_0$ comes from.




#### Exercise 2 - Recovering the same equation from Cartesian form

> [!question]
> Start with $\left(x-x_0\right)^2+\left(y-y_0\right)^2=R^2$ and substitute
> 
> $$
> x=\rho \cos \varphi, \quad y=\rho \sin \varphi, \quad x_0=\rho_0 \cos \varphi_0, \quad y_0=\rho_0 \sin \varphi_0
> $$
> 
> 
> Use $\cos \varphi \cos \varphi_0+\sin \varphi \sin \varphi_0=\cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)$ to reduce to the general polar equation. Explain why no further trigonometric expansion is necessary.


#### Exercise 3 - Rotational transparency (invariance under global rotation)

> [!question]
> Show that replacing $\left(\varphi, \varphi_0\right) \mapsto\left(\varphi+\alpha, \varphi_0+\alpha\right)$ leaves
> 
> $$
> \rho^2-2 \rho \rho_0 \cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)+\rho_0^2
> $$
> 
> unchanged. Interpret this as "only the angle difference matters," i.e., the circle is invariant under a common rotation of points and center.




#### Exercise 4 - Specialization to a center on the polar axis

> [!question]
> Set $\varphi_0=0$ in the general formula and simplify to obtain
> 
> $$
> \rho^2-2 \rho \rho_0 \cos \varphi+\rho_0^2=R^2
> $$
> 
> 
> Explain geometrically why $\varphi_0=0$ corresponds to placing the center at ( $\rho_0, 0$ ).



#### Exercise 5 - Passing through the pole and the canonical form

> [!question]
> Impose the condition that the circle passes through the origin by substituting $\rho=0$. Prove $\rho_0^2=R^2$. For a center on the positive axis take $\rho_0=R$ and factor
> 
> $$
> \rho^2-2 R \rho \cos \varphi+R^2-R^2=\rho(\rho-2 R \cos \varphi)=0
> $$
> 
> then argue that the circle is described by the nontrivial branch $\rho=2 R \cos \varphi$.



#### Exercise 6 - Signed radius and coverage of the full circle

> [!question]
> Assume the convention allowing signed $\rho$ with $(\rho, \varphi) \sim(-\rho, \varphi+\pi)$. Prove that $\rho=2 R \cos \varphi$ traces the entire circle exactly once as $\varphi$ ranges over any interval of length $2 \pi$. Then prove that restricting to $\rho \geq 0$ limits the trace to $|\varphi| \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$, and explain how the equivalence $(\rho, \varphi) \sim(-\rho, \varphi+\pi)$ recovers the hidden half.



#### Exercise 7 - Enclosing the pole, tangent at the pole

> [!question]
> For a circle with center $\rho_0$ and radius $R$, show: the pole lies inside the circle iff $R>\rho_0$, on the circle iff $R=\rho_0$, and outside iff $R<\rho_0$. In the touching case $R=\rho_0$, prove that the tangent at the pole is perpendicular to the polar axis by differentiating $\rho=2 R \cos \varphi$ at $\varphi= \pm \frac{\pi}{2}$ and converting to the Cartesian slope at the origin.






#### Exercise 8 - Solving the quadratic for $\rho(\varphi)$ and two-valuedness

> [!question]
> Treat $\rho$ as the unknown in
> 
> $$
> \rho^2-2 \rho \rho_0 \cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)+\left(\rho_0^2-R^2\right)=0
> $$
> 
> and solve explicitly:
> 
> $$
> \rho(\varphi)=\rho_0 \cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right) \pm \sqrt{R^2-\rho_0^2 \sin ^2\left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)}
> $$
> 
> 
> Determine precisely for which $\varphi$ the square root is real and explain geometrically why two branches appear (front/back relative to the pole).



#### Exercise 9 - Back-out the Cartesian center and radius from polar data

> [!question]
> Given $\left(\rho_0, \varphi_0, R\right)$, prove that the Cartesian center is $C=\left(x_0, y_0\right)=\left(\rho_0 \cos \varphi_0, \rho_0 \sin \varphi_0\right)$ and that substituting these into $\left(x-x_0\right)^2+\left(y-y_0\right)^2=R^2$ is equivalent to the polar equation. Conclude that the two descriptions are completely interchangeable.



#### Exercise 10 - Numerical identity and stability

> [!question]
> Expand $\cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)$ into $\cos \varphi \cos \varphi_0+\sin \varphi \sin \varphi_0$ and show that substituting this into the general polar equation reproduces the Cartesian expression after converting $(\rho, \varphi)$ and $\left(\rho_0, \varphi_0\right)$ to $(x, y)$ and ( $x_0, y_0$ ). Then discuss (briefly, but concretely) why evaluating $\cos \left(\varphi-\varphi_0\right)$ directly can reduce cancellation compared with separately forming $\cos \varphi \cos \varphi_0+\sin \varphi \sin \varphi_0$ when $\varphi \approx \varphi_0$.



#### Exercise 11 - Visibility intervals and extrema of $\rho$

> [!question]
> For $\varphi_0=0$ show that the extrema of $\rho(\varphi)$ occur at $\varphi=0$ and $\varphi=\pi$ with values $\rho_{\max }=\rho_0+R$ and $\rho_{\text {min }}=\left|\rho_0-R\right|$. Interpret these as farthest/nearest points of the circle from the pole, and relate $\rho_{\text {min }}=$ 0 to the touching case $R=\rho_0$.




#### Exercise 12 - Symmetry about the line $\varphi=\varphi_0$

> [!question]
> Prove that $\rho\left(\varphi_0+\delta\right)=\rho\left(\varphi_0-\delta\right)$ for all $\delta$ satisfying the reality condition in Exercise 8 , showing the circle is symmetric about the ray at angle $\varphi_0$ from the pole.
